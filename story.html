Sarah's story: “I left knowing something was finally being done.”

Capt. Sarah Macdonald (Ret’d) served for fourteen years. She spent nearly as long avoiding the VAC process. This is what changed.

Video · 4 min Recorded 2026 Canadian Armed Forces Veteran

“I'd heard the stories. Friends who applied and waited two years for a no. I decided early on that I wasn't going to put myself through that.”

Sarah released from the Canadian Armed Forces with a back injury from a training exercise and a hearing loss she'd stopped mentioning to anyone. For years she managed both on her own. When her partner suggested applying to Veterans Affairs Canada, she'd change the subject.

“It wasn't that I didn't think I qualified,” she says. “It was the paperwork. The idea of pulling my medical file, tracking down records from a base that had closed, and then explaining it all to a stranger. I didn't have the energy for it.”

The first conversation

Sarah's first contact with 270 West was a thirty-minute call she nearly cancelled. What she remembers most is that nobody asked her to prove anything.

“They asked me to tell them about my service. Not the injury, the service. Where I'd been posted, what I'd done. And then they listened. That was the first time I felt like the story was mine and not a form somebody else was filling out.”

“I came with no expectations. I left knowing something was finally being done.”

Building the file

Over the next few weeks, her advisor put together a checklist of what VAC would need, in the order it made sense to gather it. Sarah's job was to sign release forms and answer questions. The rest, including the records from the closed base, was handled for her.

“Every week I knew exactly where things stood. When VAC came back with a question, I didn't panic. I forwarded it and we talked it through.”

What she'd tell other veterans

“Don't wait as long as I did. You don't have to have it figured out. You just have to be willing to tell your story to someone who'll listen properly. The rest can be worked out.”
