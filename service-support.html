Situations

When the next VAC step comes, we are there with you

A new question can arise while you wait for a decision, after a claim is settled or when your family’s needs change.

Situation 01

“I got my decision letter. What does it mean?”

We go through the letter with you, explain what VAC decided and talk about what may come next.

Situation 02

“I need help finding a health-care provider.”

We connect you with a professional suited to the assessment or treatment you need and coordinate the next step where we can.

Situation 03

“Getting to appointments is difficult.”

We help coordinate transportation to medical appointments and assessments, so logistics do not slow your claim down.

Situation 04

“My partner is a veteran, and I am trying to help.”

We can explain general steps to family members. With the veteran’s permission, we help work through the details of their situation together.

Situation 05

“I need to understand the Caregiver Recognition Benefit.”

We help the veteran and their caregiver understand the program and prepare their parts of the application if they decide to apply.

Situation 06

“I am not sure who to contact at VAC.”

We help you work out whom to ask and prepare your questions. With the appropriate authorization, we can obtain information from VAC about your account and help you understand it.

Our approach

How we provide ongoing support for veterans

The help you need may change over time. We focus on the question or task in front of you and are available when another one arises.

01
Decision letter walkthroughs
We explain VAC letters and program information in plain language, so you know what needs attention and what choices you have.
02
Connect you with healthcare providers
We connect you with experienced medical professionals familiar with VAC assessments, treatment and documentation, and help coordinate appointments where appropriate.
03
Transportation coordination
We help arrange transport to medical and VAC appointments, which removes one more barrier.
04
Connections to external legal support
For issues outside 270 West’s services, such as overpayments or clawbacks, we can connect you with external lawyers who can provide legal advice.
05
Family, caregiver & authorized support
With your permission, we can include a spouse, family member, caregiver or person acting under a Power of Attorney in conversations and help them understand relevant VAC programs and next steps.
06
Check-ins between milestones
We schedule quick conversations with you to plan, decompress or decide what is next.
07
Community and practical support
Not every need is a VAC form. We can help connect veterans and families with relevant community resources, including housing, food support, mental health services and other practical guidance when needed.
Between the milestones

Much of the complexity sits between the major stages of a claim: finding the right healthcare professional, arranging transportation, understanding a letter, preparing a question for VAC or working out what should happen next.

That practical support is an important part of how we work.

Questions about ongoing support for veterans and families

Can I contact you after VAC has decided my claim?

Yes. We can help you understand the decision letter, look at relevant programs and work through the next practical steps. If you disagree with the decision, we can also help you understand the review and appeal processes.

Can my spouse or another family member speak with you?

Yes. Our support for veterans includes explaining general information to family members. To discuss a veteran’s personal case or act on their behalf, we first confirm the veteran’s permission and any authorization needed.

Can 270 West get information from VAC about my account?

Yes. With the appropriate authorization, 270 West can obtain information from VAC about your account and help you understand it. We can help you prepare questions or information you may need to provide, but we do not relay information to VAC on your behalf.

Who applies for the Caregiver Recognition Benefit?

The veteran applies and designates an informal caregiver. Both the veteran and caregiver complete and sign their respective parts of the application. We can help you understand the steps and what information may be needed.
