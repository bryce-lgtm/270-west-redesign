VAC Reviews & Appeals support for Canadian veterans

If you receive a VAC disability benefits decision you disagree with, there are different review routes depending on the decision and the information available. We help you understand the decision, identify gaps, coordinate supporting information and prepare for the next step. If the matter proceeds to the Veterans Review and Appeal Board (VRAB), we work within the direction of your legal representative to help gather supporting information where appropriate.

Situations

When veterans come to us for VAC Reviews & Appeals help

Situation 01

“I received a decision and do not know what to do next.”

We go through the decision with you, explain the available review routes, help identify gaps in the supporting information and connect you with resources or assessments that may help address them.

Situation 02

“My assessment seems low. What route should I look at?”

We look at what you disagree with and what has changed. Depending on the circumstances, a reassessment or a departmental review may be the more relevant route. Our experience helps us explain the distinction and what information each route may require.

Situation 03

“My VAC decision has already been reviewed. What can I do next?”

If a departmental review does not resolve the issue, the next route may be through the Veterans Review and Appeal Board. We help you understand the stage you are at and connect you with the appropriate legal representation and resources.

Our approach

How we help with VAC Reviews & Appeals

A review or appeal is not a fight. It is a structured opportunity to look again at a decision with the right supporting information. We start with the decision, identify the gaps and help you understand whether the next route is a VAC departmental review or a VRAB proceeding.

Questions about VAC Reviews & Appeals

Is a departmental review the same as a Veterans Review and Appeal Board (VRAB) appeal?

No. VAC conducts a departmental review of its own decision, often considering new information. VRAB is an independent tribunal. Its first stage is a review hearing. An appeal hearing may follow if you disagree with the Board’s review decision.

Can I seek a review if VAC approved my claim but I disagree with the assessment?

Yes, a disability assessment can be reviewed or appealed. If your condition has worsened since the assessment, you may instead need a reassessment. The distinction depends on what has changed and which decision you want examined.

Is it too late to seek a review or appeal of my disability benefits decision?

A delay does not automatically mean there is no route available. Depending on the decision and the stage of the file, you may be able to request a VAC departmental review or proceed to VRAB. We can help you identify where your matter sits and the information you may need; the Bureau of Pensions Advocates can provide free legal advice about a VRAB review or appeal.

Do I need a lawyer for a VRAB review or appeal?

Free legal advice and representation are available through the Bureau of Pensions Advocates, a national team of lawyers within VAC that represents veterans in disability benefit reviews and appeals. If your matter proceeds to VRAB, your lawyer will advise you on the case and what evidence is needed. We can help you understand how to connect with that support and, where appropriate, help gather information your lawyer identifies.
