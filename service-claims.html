Situations

When veterans come to us for VAC claims help

Whether you are starting for the first time or a claim has stalled, become confusing or hit a roadblock, we can help you work out what is needed to move forward.

Situation 01

“I have never applied before, and I do not know where to start.”

We explain the first steps and talk through what you may need. You do not have to gather every document before speaking with us.

Situation 02

“I started a claim and got stuck.”

We review what you have completed, identify what is still needed and build a practical roadmap to help you get the claim moving again.

Situation 03

“I am not sure I qualify.”

Many veterans may qualify for more than they realize. We talk through your service, your current situation and the benefits that may be relevant.

Situation 04

“I am not sure whether my injury or condition is related to my service.”

We look at your service history, trade, relevant events and available medical information. Where useful, we research historical context and relevant precedent to help you understand whether a service connection may be worth exploring. VAC makes the entitlement decision.

Situation 05

“I have an injury, but I have been out of the service for decades.”

Time since release does not automatically mean there is nothing to explore. We can help you look at your service history, current diagnosis and available records to understand what information a claim may need and what route may be relevant.

Our approach

How we help with VAC claims

The basic stages of a VAC claim are straightforward to describe. The details within them vary from one veteran to another. We work out what your claim may need, bring the information together and review the claim with you before it goes to VAC.

01
Understand your options
We look at your service history and circumstances to identify the VAC disability benefits that may be relevant to your claim.
02
Work out what information is needed
We explain what details you can provide, what may need to come from a health-care professional and what VAC obtains directly.
03
Research the context and prepare the claim with you
We bring together the information, draw on experience with similar trades and service circumstances, research relevant historical context and precedent where useful, and prepare the claim with your input. You review and approve the material before filing.
04
Support filing, follow-up and next steps
We help you get the claim ready to file and remain involved if VAC asks for more information. When a decision arrives, we explain what it means, what still needs attention and the next steps that may keep the process moving.
Components of a strong claim

Components of strong VAC claims

A well-supported VAC claim brings together detailed service history, a clear current description of the condition and medical documentation, and a credible connection between the two. Our experience across thousands of claims, including trade and historical context and relevant precedent, helps us identify where details may need closer attention.

  1. 01

    Detailed service history

    Relevant details about your service and the events or experiences connected with your claim.

  2. 02

    Complete documentation

    A current diagnosis and medical records that identify and describe your condition.

  3. 03

    Credible connections

    An explanation of how your condition relates to your service. We help make sure the description of how your condition relates to your service is clear, specific and supported by the available information.

Questions about filing a VAC claim

Can I speak with 270 West if I have not been diagnosed yet?

Yes. A Benefit Navigator can talk through your situation and what a claim may need. VAC requires a diagnosed condition before it can decide entitlement to disability benefits, and the diagnosis comes from a health-care professional. We can help you arrange that assessment.

What is an applicant statement?

It is your opportunity to explain how your condition relates to your service. We help you present the relevant details clearly, and you review the statement before it is filed.

Who completes a VAC medical questionnaire?

A doctor or other health-care professional completes and signs the relevant questionnaire. We help you understand where it fits in the claim and what you need to follow up on.

Do I need to request my Canadian Armed Forces service health records?

VAC may request service and health records directly as part of a claim. Veterans can also request their own military records from the Department of National Defence or Library and Archives Canada, depending on where the records are held. We can help you work out which records may be useful and where to request them.

Can I apply for more than one condition?

Yes. VAC requires each condition to be addressed separately in the application. We help keep the description, medical information and service connection for each condition clear.
