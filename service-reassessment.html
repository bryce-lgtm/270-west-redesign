Situations

When a VAC reassessment may be the next step

Your health can change after VAC has assessed a service-related condition. We help you understand whether reassessment or another application route fits your situation.

Situation 01

“My condition has gotten worse.”

A reassessment may be appropriate when an already entitled condition has become more severe and current medical information can show the change.

Situation 02

“I have a new diagnosis related to my service.”

A new diagnosis may require a new disability benefits claim rather than a reassessment. We help you understand which route may apply.

Situation 03

“I’m not sure a reassessment makes sense for me.”

We review your current assessment, what has changed and the information available before giving you a straightforward view of your options.

What VAC considers

What VAC considers during a reassessment

VAC reassesses the current extent of the condition for which you already receive disability benefits. The information required depends on the condition and the changes you report.

  1. 01

    Current medical findings

    Clinical information from a doctor or other health-care professional can show how the condition has changed.

  2. 02

    Severity of the condition

    VAC considers the current level of impairment associated with the entitled condition.

  3. 03

    Effect on your quality of life

    The assessment may consider how the condition now affects activities at home, work and in the community.

A VAC reassessment does not guarantee a higher rating. VAC reviews the information and makes the decision.

Common questions

Questions about VAC reassessments

What is a VAC reassessment?

A VAC reassessment is a new evaluation of the severity of a condition for which you already receive disability benefits. You can request one when the condition has worsened and medical information can show the change.

Is a VAC reassessment the same as a departmental review?

No. A VAC reassessment looks at how an entitled condition has changed since it was assessed. A departmental review asks VAC to look again at an earlier decision. The right route depends on the decision, the information available, and when the change occurred.

What if I have developed a new condition?

A new condition may require a new disability benefits application, even when you believe it is related to an already entitled condition. We can help you distinguish a new claim from a reassessment.

What medical information may be needed?

VAC may need current clinical information describing the severity of your condition and its effect on your quality of life. The specific reports or questionnaires depend on the condition and the information already available.

Does a VAC reassessment guarantee a higher rating?

No. VAC decides whether the information confirms that your condition has worsened. If it does, VAC may adjust your disability benefit unless you are already receiving the maximum amount.

What happens if VAC asks for more information?

We help you understand the request, identify who needs to provide the information and prepare your response.
