Why 270 West for veteran benefits navigation

Experience changes what you see.

The VAC process has defined stages. Every veteran’s service, health history and circumstances are different.

Experience across more than 1,700 clients and thousands of claims helps us know which questions are worth asking, where a closer look may be useful, how the pieces may connect and what needs to happen next.

The process is not the advantage. Experience is.

A form tells you what fields to complete. It does not tell you which part of a long service history deserves more attention, whether a medical assessment may add useful context, how a previous decision affects the next route, or which task needs to happen first.

That is where experience matters: not as a shortcut or a guarantee, but as judgement built from seeing many different service histories, health circumstances and VAC routes.

What experience changes

  1. 01

    Know what to ask

    Many veterans begin with one condition, one letter or one question. We start with the broader context — your service, trade, health history, previous VAC activity and what you are trying to resolve. The right questions can reveal information that deserves attention without assuming your situation is the same as someone else’s.

  2. 02

    Know where to look

    Experience helps us recognize when service history, medical information, prior decisions or current circumstances may need a closer review. Where useful, we research relevant policy and precedent and identify information that may help clarify the picture.

  3. 03

    Know how the pieces connect

    A claim can involve service records, medical assessments, documentation, different VAC routes, appointments and follow-up. We help connect those pieces into a coherent roadmap rather than treating each task as a separate form.

  4. 04

    Know what comes next

    Not every task needs to happen at once. We help sequence the work, track what is outstanding and explain VAC updates and decisions so you know what needs attention now and what may come later.

Why experience matters

Veterans describe the difference between knowing the steps and knowing what to notice.

Every veteran still starts with their own facts.

Experience is useful because it gives us context — not because it turns a veteran’s situation into a template.

We may have supported someone with a similar trade, injury, deployment or VAC route, but we do not assume the same answer applies to you. We listen, verify what is relevant and build the work around your circumstances.

Knowing when another expert is needed is part of the work.

270 West does not try to be every expert. When medical assessment or documentation is needed, we connect clients with experienced health-care professionals where appropriate. When an issue falls outside our services, we help identify the right external resource.

Our role is to understand how those pieces fit into the broader benefits journey and help keep the work connected.

What our expertise does not mean

  • It does not mean guaranteed approval. VAC makes decisions about eligibility and benefits.
  • It does not mean special access to VAC or inside information.
  • It does not mean taking control of your claim. You review the information, ask questions and decide what goes to VAC.
  • It does not mean free support is the wrong choice. Veterans have different needs, and we explain the alternatives available.

We believe expertise is most useful when it is paired with transparency, restraint and respect for the veteran’s choices.
