Frequently asked questions about VAC claims and 270 West Consulting

Things veterans ask us most.

Answers to the most common questions about Veterans Affairs Canada benefits, our services, pricing, and process.

About 270 West

Is 270 West Consulting part of Veterans Affairs Canada?

No. We're an independent, Canadian-owned company. We help veterans put together strong applications, and we're not employed by or affiliated with VAC.

Where is 270 West based?

We’re headquartered in Halifax, Nova Scotia, with offices in communities across Atlantic Canada and Ontario. Wherever you are in Canada, our team can support you — in person, by phone or virtually, depending on what works best for you.

Are you accredited?

Yes. We're BBB Accredited and a member of recognized Canadian veteran-services networks.

Will I still make decisions about my claim?

Yes. We take on preparation, organization and follow-up, but it remains your claim. We review the information with you and seek your approval before it is submitted.

Can my family or caregiver be involved?

Yes, if you want them involved. Partners, family members and caregivers can play an important role in helping you manage appointments, information and decisions.

Does 270 West charge for all its support?

No. Many of the supports and resources we provide are free. Our fee for benefits navigation and filing is 10%, payable only if your claim is successful. Conversations with our team are always free, and we explain our fees and terms before you decide whether to work with us.

Pricing & process

Is the VAC Status Checker really free?

Yes. The check is free, and so are conversations with our team. If you'd like our help with a full application, we explain pricing before you decide.

How long does the process take?

The VAC status check is about two minutes. From there, timelines depend on your situation and on VAC's review process. We set realistic expectations together in our first meeting.

What does 270 West actually do during a claim?

We help you gather documentation, complete forms, and submit a clear, complete VAC application. We stay with you through any follow-up VAC may need.

VAC programs

What VAC programs can I apply for?

There are several main programs: Disability Benefits, Income Replacement Benefit, Career Transition Services, and Caregiver Recognition. See our Resources page for guides on each.

What if my VAC claim was already denied?

We can still help. Sometimes a fresh look at the documentation or framing of a claim makes a meaningful difference. Bring us your story and we'll walk through it together.

Can I apply for VAC benefits on my own?

Yes. VAC accepts applications directly from veterans, and many people apply on their own. We're here if you'd like hands-on help.

Privacy & confidentiality

Is the information I share confidential?

Yes. Everything you share with us is treated as confidential. We don't share your information with third parties without your written consent.

Do you keep my records?

We maintain client records securely and only for as long as necessary to provide our services. See our Privacy Policy for details.

Filing a VAC claim

Can I speak with 270 West if I haven’t been diagnosed yet?

Yes. A benefits navigator can talk through your situation and what an application may need. VAC requires a diagnosed condition before it can decide entitlement to disability benefits, and the diagnosis comes from a health-care professional. We can help you arrange that assessment.

What is an applicant statement?

It is your opportunity to explain how your condition relates to your service. We help you present the relevant details clearly, and you review the statement before submission.

Who completes a VAC medical questionnaire?

A doctor or other health-care professional completes and signs the relevant questionnaire. We help you understand where it fits in the application and what you need to follow up on.

Do I need to request my Canadian Armed Forces service health records?

VAC requests those records directly from the Department of National Defence or Library and Archives Canada. We can help you identify other information that may be useful to include.

Can I apply for more than one condition?

Yes. VAC recommends a separate account of each condition in the application. We help keep the details for each condition clear.

VAC appeals, reviews and reconsideration

Is a departmental review the same as a Veterans Review and Appeal Board (VRAB) appeal?

No. VAC conducts a departmental review of its own decision, often considering new information. VRAB is an independent tribunal. Its first stage is a review hearing. An appeal hearing may follow if you disagree with the Board’s review decision.

What does “VAC reconsideration” mean?

The terms can be confusing. When VAC looks again at one of its disability benefit decisions, the formal route is generally called a departmental review. A VRAB reconsideration is different. It is a possible reopening of a final Board appeal decision where new, credible and relevant information becomes available, or there was an error of fact or law. We help identify which decision you have before discussing the next step.

Can I seek a review if VAC approved my claim but I disagree with the assessment?

Yes, a disability assessment can be reviewed or appealed. If your condition has worsened since the assessment, you may instead need a reassessment. The distinction depends on what has changed and which decision you want examined.

Is it too late to appeal my disability benefits decision?

A long gap does not automatically rule out a VRAB review or VAC appeal. Other legal steps can have strict deadlines, so it is important to know which decision you received and what stage your case is at. Bring the letters you have, and we can help you identify the appropriate source of advice.

Do I need a lawyer for a VRAB hearing?

You may represent yourself. The Bureau of Pensions Advocates offers free legal representation. You may choose a private representative as well. We can help you understand those options.

VAC reassessments

What is a VAC reassessment?

A VAC reassessment is a new evaluation of the severity of a condition for which you already receive disability benefits. You can request one when the condition has worsened and medical information can show the change.

Is a VAC reassessment the same as a departmental review?

No. A VAC reassessment looks at how an entitled condition has changed since it was assessed. A departmental review asks VAC to look again at an earlier decision. The right route depends on the decision, the information available, and when the change occurred.

What if I have developed a new condition?

A new condition may require a new disability benefits application, even when you believe it is related to an already entitled condition. We can help you distinguish a new claim from a reassessment.

What medical information may be needed?

VAC may need current clinical information describing the severity of your condition and its effect on your quality of life. The specific reports or questionnaires depend on the condition and the information already available.

Does a VAC reassessment guarantee a higher assessment?

No. VAC decides whether the information confirms that your condition has worsened. If it does, VAC may adjust your disability benefit unless you are already receiving the maximum amount.

What happens if VAC asks for more information?

We help you understand the request, identify who needs to provide the information and prepare your response.

Ongoing support for veterans and families

Can I contact you after VAC has decided my claim?

Yes. We can help you understand the decision letter, look at relevant programs and work through the next practical steps. If you disagree with the decision, we can also help you understand the review and appeal processes.

Can my spouse or another family member speak with you?

Yes. Our support for veterans includes explaining general information to family members. To discuss a veteran’s personal case or act on their behalf, we first confirm the veteran’s permission and any authorization needed.

Can you speak to VAC on my behalf?

With appropriate authorization, we can help prepare questions and assist with follow-up. We keep you informed and involved in decisions about your case.

Who applies for the Caregiver Recognition Benefit?

The veteran applies and designates an informal caregiver. Both the veteran and caregiver complete and sign their respective parts of the application. We can help you understand the steps and what information may be needed.

Working with us

Do I need to use the online status checker first?

No. You can book a conversation directly. The short online form is an optional starting point.

What if I have already started my VAC claim application?

We look at what you have completed and what remains. Then we explain where our help could be useful. You do not have to start over simply because you contacted us partway through.

What if I have already received a VAC decision?

Bring the decision letter to a conversation with us. We can help you understand what VAC decided and discuss the routes that may still be available.

Can I apply on my own or use free support?

Yes. You can apply directly through VAC, and other organizations offer support. Our service is an option if you want more hands-on help with preparation, coordination and follow-up.

Can someone in my family take part?

Yes. A person you trust can join the conversation. We confirm your permission before discussing your personal case with them or acting on your behalf.

Can I keep working with other organizations or professionals?

Absolutely. We respect the support you already have and, with your permission, can coordinate with others where helpful.

Can you communicate with VAC for me?

With the appropriate authorization, we can help prepare questions and assist with follow-up on your VAC claims. We keep you informed and involved in decisions about your claim.

What is 270 West’s relationship with Veterans Affairs Canada?

Our approach is collaborative. VAC administers benefits and services for veterans, while 270 West helps veterans understand their options, prepare their claims and navigate the process. We share a common goal: helping veterans get the support and services available to them.

Our services

What exactly does 270 West do?

We help Canadian veterans and their families understand and navigate the Veterans Affairs Canada disability benefits process. That can include understanding your options, organizing the information a claim needs, preparing and filing documentation, connecting you with medical professionals and resources, and keeping you informed about what comes next.

How much does 270 West charge?

Our fee for benefits navigation and filing is 10%, and we only get paid if your claim is successful. Conversations with our team are always free, and our benefits navigators explain our fees and terms clearly before you decide whether to move forward.

Does 270 West charge for everything it does?

No. Many of the supports and resources we provide are at no charge. The service we charge for is benefits navigation and filing.

Is there any commitment required for the first meeting?

No. The first meeting is a conversation. We listen, answer questions and explain where we may be able to help. Then you decide whether there is a fit.

Does 270 West take over my claim?

No, and that distinction matters. Our benefits navigators take on the administrative work, organization and follow-up, but it remains your claim. You review the information, ask questions and approve what is submitted.

Can 270 West guarantee my VAC claim will be approved?

No responsible organization can guarantee how VAC will decide a claim. What we can promise is that we take your experience seriously, prepare the information carefully and support you through the process.

Is 270 West part of Veterans Affairs Canada?

No. We are an independent Canadian company and are not affiliated with Veterans Affairs Canada.

The VAC status checker

Who qualifies for veterans benefits in Canada?

The requirements depend on the benefit. For VAC disability benefits, factors include your service, a diagnosed condition and its connection to your service. Other benefits have their own requirements. Speak with us to learn more about disability benefit criteria.

Am I eligible for veterans benefits?

This check cannot determine your eligibility. A benefits navigator can review what you share, discuss services that may be relevant to your situation and help you understand possible next steps.

Can I use the check if I’ve already received a VAC decision?

Yes. You can use it if you want to discuss a decision, are considering an appeal or have a condition that has changed since your assessment.

Will this show the status of a claim I’ve submitted to VAC?

No. The check does not connect to My VAC Account or retrieve updates on an application. It helps us understand where you are in the process so we can have a useful follow-up conversation.
