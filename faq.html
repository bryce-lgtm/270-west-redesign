Frequently asked questions about VAC claims and 270 West Consulting

Things veterans ask us most.

Answers to the most common questions about Veterans Affairs Canada benefits, our services, pricing, and process.

About 270 West

Is 270 West Consulting part of Veterans Affairs Canada?

No. 270 West is an independent, Canadian-owned company with roots in veterans helping veterans navigate the VAC process. We work collaboratively within the system and help veterans understand their options, prepare their claim information and navigate next steps. VAC independently makes decisions about benefits and eligibility.

Where is 270 West based?

We are headquartered in Halifax, Nova Scotia, with offices in communities across Atlantic Canada and Ontario. Wherever you are in Canada, our team can support you by phone or virtually.

Are you accredited?

Yes. We are BBB Accredited.

What experience does 270 West have with VAC claims?

Our team has supported more than 1,700 clients through thousands of claims. That experience gives us a broad understanding of a process that can involve different application routes, service and medical information, follow-up requests and decisions. It helps us know what questions to ask, what information may be relevant and where a claim may need closer attention. Every claim is considered individually, and VAC makes all decisions about eligibility and benefits.

Can 270 West connect me with a doctor or other health-care professional?

We can connect clients with health-care professionals for medical assessments and treatment related to their claim where appropriate. Whether a practitioner can take someone on as an ongoing patient depends on that practitioner’s availability and capacity.

Will I still make decisions about my claim?

Yes. We take on preparation, organization and follow-up, but it remains your claim. We review the information with you, and you decide what will go to VAC. When it is ready, we help you file it.

Can my family or caregiver be involved?

Yes, if you want them involved. Partners, family members and caregivers can play an important role in helping you manage appointments, information and decisions.

Pricing & process

How do 270 West’s fees work?

Conversations with our team are always free. If you choose to work with us on a claim, our fee is 10% on a successful claim. The fee reflects the full support we provide: research, preparation, appointment coordination, documentation, helping you file, tracking what is outstanding, explaining VAC updates and decisions, and helping you understand benefits and supports. You only pay if the claim is successful, and we explain the fee and terms before you decide to proceed.

Does 270 West charge for all its support?

No. The 10% success fee applies to the claim support described above. Some other guidance and resources are available at no charge, and we explain what is included before you decide whether to work with us.

Is the VAC Status Checker really free?

Yes. The Status Checker and conversations with our team are free. If you later choose to work with us on a claim, we explain the 10% success fee, what it covers and the terms before you decide whether to proceed.

How long does the process take?

The VAC status check is about two minutes. From there, timelines depend on your situation and on VAC's review process. We set realistic expectations together in our first meeting.

What exactly does 270 West do?

We help Canadian veterans and their families understand and navigate VAC disability benefits. That can include understanding your options, organizing the information a claim needs, preparing the paperwork with you, connecting you with medical professionals and resources, helping you get ready to file, and supporting you through VAC’s decision and what may come next.

Some veterans choose 270 West because the process can involve different routes, records, medical information, appointments and follow-up. Our team brings experience from thousands of claims and can coordinate the practical work while keeping you informed and in control.

Filing a VAC claim

Can I speak with 270 West if I have not been diagnosed yet?

Yes. A Benefit Navigator can talk through your situation and what a claim may need. VAC requires a diagnosed condition before it can decide entitlement to disability benefits, and the diagnosis comes from a health-care professional. We can help you arrange that assessment.

What is an applicant statement?

It is your opportunity to explain how your condition relates to your service. We help you present the relevant details clearly, and you review and approve the statement before we help you send it to VAC.

Who completes a VAC medical questionnaire?

A doctor or other health-care professional completes and signs the relevant questionnaire. We help you understand where it fits in the claim and what you need to follow up on.

Do I need to request my Canadian Armed Forces service health records?

No. Most claims can move forward without you personally obtaining your full service health records. VAC can request service health records from the Department of National Defence or Library and Archives Canada. If additional records are needed, we can help you understand what to request and where to request it.

Can I apply for more than one condition?

Yes. VAC requires each condition to be addressed separately in the application. We help keep the description, medical information and service connection for each condition clear.

VAC Reviews & Appeals

Is a departmental review the same as a Veterans Review and Appeal Board (VRAB) appeal?

No. VAC conducts a departmental review of its own decision, often considering new information. VRAB is an independent tribunal. Its first stage is a review hearing. An appeal hearing may follow if you disagree with the Board’s review decision.

Can I seek a review if VAC approved my claim but I disagree with the assessment?

Yes, a disability assessment can be reviewed or appealed. If your condition has worsened since the assessment, you may instead need a reassessment. The distinction depends on what has changed and which decision you want examined.

Is it too late to seek a review or appeal of my disability benefits decision?

No. For VAC disability benefit decisions, there is no time limit to request a review of the Department’s decision. If the matter proceeds to VRAB, the first Board stage is a Review Hearing, followed by an Appeal Hearing if you disagree with the Review decision. Bring the decision letters you have and we can help you understand which route you are at.

Do I need a lawyer for a VRAB hearing?

Veterans have access to free legal representation through the Bureau of Pensions Advocates for VRAB hearings. A BPA lawyer can advise you, prepare the case and represent you before the Board. Other representation options exist, but the free BPA service is the primary option to explain first. 270 West can help you gather supporting information requested for the case.

VAC reassessments

What is a VAC reassessment?

A VAC reassessment is a new evaluation of the severity of a condition VAC has already recognized. A condition can be entitled even if the earlier assessment was 0% or did not result in a payment. A reassessment may be appropriate when the condition has worsened and current medical information can show the change.

Is a VAC reassessment the same as a departmental review?

No. A VAC reassessment looks at how an entitled condition has changed since it was assessed. A departmental review asks VAC to look again at an earlier decision. The right route depends on the decision, the information available, and when the change occurred.

What if I have developed a new condition?

A new condition may require a new disability benefits application, even when you believe it is related to an already entitled condition. We can help you distinguish a new claim from a reassessment.

What medical information may be needed?

VAC may need a current medical assessment or other clinical information describing the severity of your condition and its impact on your quality of life. The specific reports or questionnaires depend on the condition and the information already available.

Does a VAC reassessment guarantee a higher assessment?

No. VAC decides whether the current information supports a higher assessment. A reassessment can affect the level of disability benefits and may also be relevant to treatment coverage connected with the entitled condition.

What happens if VAC asks for more information?

We help you understand what VAC is asking for, identify who needs to provide the information and, where medical information is needed, connect you with an appropriate provider for an assessment or documentation.

Ongoing support for veterans and families

Can I contact you after VAC has decided my claim?

Yes. We can help you understand the decision letter, look at relevant programs and work through the next practical steps. If you disagree with the decision, we can also help you understand the review and appeal processes.

Can my spouse or another family member speak with you?

Yes. Our support for veterans includes explaining general information to family members. To discuss a veteran’s personal case or act on their behalf, we first confirm the veteran’s permission and any authorization needed.

Can 270 West get information from VAC about my account?

Yes. With the appropriate authorization, 270 West can obtain information from VAC about your account and help you understand it. We can help you prepare questions or information you may need to provide, but we do not relay information to VAC on your behalf.

Who applies for the Caregiver Recognition Benefit?

The veteran applies and designates an informal caregiver. Both the veteran and caregiver complete and sign their respective parts of the application. We can help you understand the steps and what information may be needed.

The VAC status checker

Who can qualify for VAC benefits?

VAC benefits may be available to serving or former Canadian Armed Forces members, eligible RCMP members and, for some programs, family members or survivors. For disability benefits, VAC generally looks at whether there is a diagnosed condition and a connection between that condition and service. Eligibility varies by program and type of service. We can help you understand which programs may be relevant; VAC makes the eligibility decision.

What benefits am I eligible for?

Based on your service and what you share with us, a 270 West team member can discuss VAC benefits and supports that may be relevant and explain what each one generally requires. The online checker is a starting point for that conversation; VAC makes the formal eligibility decision.

Can I use the check if I have already received a VAC decision?

Yes. You can use it if you want to discuss a decision, are considering a review or appeal, or have a condition that has changed since your assessment.

What will the VAC Status Checker tell me?

The checker helps us understand your service, claim history and what you are trying to do so our team can have a more useful follow-up conversation. It does not connect to My VAC Account or retrieve live application updates.
