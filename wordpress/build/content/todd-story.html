Todd had put considerable effort into his first disability claim. He understood forms, wrote carefully and believed he had explained his shoulder injury clearly.

When the claim and subsequent efforts were unsuccessful, he eventually stopped pursuing it.

A conversation at a DND Family Days event in Halifax gave him a reason to try again.

Leaving the uniform behind

Todd joined the Canadian Armed Forces in 1984 as a supply technician. He later became a naval logistics officer and served for 33 years across the Regular Force and Reserve Force.

His career included service in six ships, overseas tours and roles with operational commands.

Leaving the navy in 2017 brought new employment and opportunities, but also a change in identity.

“The uniform is very much part of your identity.”

It had told the story of his qualifications, experiences and service. Taking it off meant finding a different way to understand where he belonged.

He also missed the everyday access to colleagues who understood military life without a long explanation.

A shoulder injury that affected everyday life

An injury sustained during officer training continued to trouble Todd. Activities as ordinary as cutting the lawn could leave him sore.

He had found that massage therapy helped and wanted support to manage the condition. He prepared a claim, obtained the requested medical information and submitted it with confidence.

The denial was deeply frustrating. He questioned whether he had explained himself properly and went back over what he had written.

After further unsuccessful attempts, he gave up for several years.

A conversation in a language he recognized

At DND Family Days, Todd stopped at the 270 West tent and spoke with our team. He later arranged a meeting with Tanya, a benefits navigator.

He noticed something early in the conversation: she understood the military language and acronyms he used.

“She got the language from the minute we started talking.”

That familiarity helped him feel comfortable. He could discuss his service and its effects without first having to explain every reference.

Todd arrived focused on his shoulder. Tanya asked about other concerns, including his knee, wrist and back. Their discussion also considered how those limitations might affect him as he grew older.

Together, they developed an action plan.

The work was shared, and the claim remained his

Instead of spending days drafting and editing alone, Todd talked through his experiences with us. We asked questions, prepared the written material and brought it back for him to review.

“I still had full control over what was going to be sent to VAC.”

We also arranged appointments with healthcare practitioners familiar with the documentation required.

“I didn’t have to coordinate. I got an appointment. I showed up.”

Todd attended the assessments and received updates as the claims progressed. Having someone organize that work reduced both the time involved and his worry about whether he had handled it correctly.

Treatment and a personal connection

Todd’s shoulder claim was subsequently approved, and he gained coverage for massage therapy. Other assessments helped him examine physical concerns he had attributed simply to getting older.

A referral for a mental health assessment also opened a conversation about the effects of his deployments. He describes the mental health support as an important part of working toward better physical and mental health.

He remembers smaller gestures, too. After a trip to Ottawa to watch his beloved Toronto Maple Leafs play, Tanya checked in with a light-hearted message following their loss.

For Todd, those moments showed that she remembered the person beyond the applications.

The relationship helped him return to a process he had abandoned, retain a voice in his claims and access support that affected his everyday life.
