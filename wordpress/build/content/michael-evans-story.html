I came to the military a bit late. I earned a business degree first, then worked as an ad exec on Bay Street with my father’s company, Evans Advertising. But I couldn’t get away from aviation. I’d been flying since I was 14. So I went to the recruiting centre and joined as a pilot. I flew Sea Kings for ten years, then C-130s, then search and rescue out of Greenwood, which turned out to be one of the most challenging kinds of flying I ever saw. I deployed whenever I could, including the Persian Gulf, Somalia, Haiti, the Balkans and Afghanistan. I finished my career in staff jobs at the Warfare Centre in Halifax, working on the Navy’s move into drones, and retired in 2022.

I never sit around. Straight out of the military I was running a real estate company and a fintech startup with my brother-in-law. I’d been meaning to fill out my Veterans Affairs paperwork for ages and never got to it. When you’re an entrepreneur, you’re working seven days a week.

Then I went to the boat show in Halifax and ran into Barry from 270 West. To be honest, my expectations were low. I hadn’t heard of the company. But he convinced me an interview was worth it, and the meeting was very low key, with no pressure at all. Through their questions, Barry and Kim came up with ideas for my application that I hadn’t considered. They knew the path I needed to go down, because they had already done it. They set up all my medical appointments, which was remarkable for someone with my schedule.

I thought I only had some hearing damage. Like a lot of people at the end of a military career, I had aches and pains I’d just accepted. The specialists they sent me to made me think about where those pains came from. I went in about my left knee, and one asked what was going on with my right, because if the left is weak, you favour the other one and wear it out. I’d never considered that. In the end I had about ten claims, including my knees, my back, my hips and tinnitus. The audiologist asked questions that made me realize how much tinnitus affects my life, things I would never have put in a form on my own. My hearing loss claim was rejected, and I’ll be using 270 West again if anything worsens, since claims stay open.

They also told me I could claim my chiropractor and massage therapist. I’d paid for those myself through my whole career, and I’d never known I could claim them.

I’m an executive summary kind of guy. I don’t need daily updates. I told Kim I only want problems or answers. When VAC opened my file, she would send me a short summary at the end of the week saying what they had looked at and what it meant, so I didn’t have to hunt it down. I trusted that she was working in my best interest.

The fee is 10% plus HST, which is clear right up front, a lot like a lawyer on contingency. For a business guy, it was a no-brainer, considering what I got out of it. When my first claim settled, I called two buddies who had done their own paperwork. They’re decent staff guys, but I’d done far better than they had. With a company that’s done this a million times, the result is predictable. Doing it yourself can be a lottery. I sent them both to 270 West.
