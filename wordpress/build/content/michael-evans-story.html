Michael Evans had been meaning to complete his Veterans Affairs Canada paperwork. But retirement had brought new responsibilities, and the forms kept slipping down his list.

After a military career spent flying, teaching, and working in operations and intelligence, he was building businesses. He needed someone who could handle the practical details and keep him informed without making the claims process another full-time commitment.

A career shaped by aviation

Michael’s interest in flying began long before he joined the military. His father took him to airshows, and he started flying as a teenager. Even after completing a business degree and working in advertising, aviation continued to draw him back.

His military career took him through Sea King helicopter operations, pilot instruction, C-130 transport and search and rescue. He deployed to several parts of the world and later moved into staff and intelligence work.

Search and rescue brought particular challenges: calls late at night, difficult weather and missions that could continue until morning. Looking back, Michael speaks with respect for the crews doing that work and the demands they carry.

When he retired in 2022, he already had a real estate business underway. A technology venture with family members soon followed. There was plenty to keep him occupied.

A conversation at a boat show

Michael first encountered 270 West at a boat show in Halifax. He spoke with Barry but knew little about us or what support we could provide.

About a month later, he arranged a meeting.

The conversation felt relaxed. Barry and Kim asked questions about his service, health and circumstances, giving him room to consider what he wanted to do.

“There was like no pressure on me.”

Michael arrived thinking mainly about hearing problems. As we talked, other concerns came into view. He began to see that we could help him work through questions he had not had time to investigate himself.

Looking more closely at familiar aches and pains

Some of Michael’s injuries had become part of everyday life. A knee injured during training had never fully stopped bothering him. Back and hip problems were things he had learned to manage.

We arranged appointments with healthcare professionals who could assess those concerns and provide the information needed for his claims.

An audiology appointment also prompted him to think differently about tinnitus. Questions about conversations in crowds and competing background noise helped him describe effects he had grown accustomed to.

Having the appointments and documentation organized made a practical difference.

“They had my back.”

Not every claim received the outcome he expected. His hearing-loss claim was not approved. Other conditions were recognized, and some matters remained ongoing at the time we spoke with him.

Updates suited the way he worked

Michael did not want a daily account of every change in his file. He preferred concise summaries explaining what had happened and what it meant.

We adapted to that preference.

“Kim made sure that I was in the loop on everything and what it was going to mean.”

Our conversations also helped him become aware of support connected to his accepted conditions, including treatment coverage he had previously been paying for himself.

He subsequently connected with a VAC caseworker whom he describes positively. Looking back on that connection, Michael says:

“They opened the door to Veterans Affairs for me.”

For Michael, the value of the relationship was practical: appointments were arranged, questions were followed through, and information arrived in a form he could use. He could keep building his next chapter while knowing that someone was attending to the details he had put off.
