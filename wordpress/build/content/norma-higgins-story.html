Ralph served for 16 years. He went overseas to Cyprus and the Golan Heights as a peacekeeper, and he was a carpenter and engineer who repaired and fixed the barracks at every base. He was calm, kind and generous, and he had a lot of friends. He wasn’t a drinker or a smoker, so he’d go to the dances and end up dancing with the other men’s wives.

We met in 1998 or 1999, volunteering at our church in Dartmouth. My sister ran the food bank and his aunt ran the clothing store, and they introduced us. We were married in 2000 and had 25 years together. He doted on me. We went for drives and visited family, and I miss him every day.

In the last year and a half, the PTSD really hit him, along with heart, kidney and bladder problems. He had just started working with Tanya on his forms when he took sick and died in November. All he talked about at home was Tanya doing this and Tanya doing that. I really don’t know who first contacted her for him. She helped us with transportation to the hospital and to his appointments, and she helped him set out his story.

After Ralph died, the help kept coming. We had to move, and we had been trying to find a place before he passed. Tanya helped us with furniture and the move itself. I can’t read the forms with my sight the way it is, so she helps me fill them out. When something arrives that says you need to fill out this form, she usually already knows what goes on it. She is helping me now with the claims Ralph had in for his hands, his feet, his PTSD, his bladder and his heart. They were all on his list before he died. VAC has told me it will take at least another nine months, so it is going to be a long, hard struggle, but I’m not doing it alone.

People told me I should have gone to the Lions because they’re free. The Lions did help me once, but I didn’t find them as friendly or as willing to help. If Tanya doesn’t know the answer, she calls and finds it and gives it back to me.

The first nine months after Ralph’s death were hard, especially the first three or four, trying to arrange the funeral. I needed people who were beside me and helping me, not just helping the officials. There were times I was so far down I could hardly move, and talking to Tanya or someone on her team brought me back up. She offers a friendly hand any time I need it.
