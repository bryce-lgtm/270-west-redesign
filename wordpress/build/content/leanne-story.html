Leanne first saw 270 West as a place she could refer other veterans. She had not considered that she might need our support herself.

As a mortgage broker, she was used to connecting people with the right help. A conversation beside a community sponsorship booth led her to look more closely at her own service and the support she had been managing without.

Three years of service, lasting effects

Leanne joined the military in 1993, shortly after leaving school. Two cousins serving in the military helped her understand the trades available and what to expect.

She arrived with ambition. She earned an athletic award during basic training and an Admiral O’Brien Award during her naval training. She believed she could do the work and build a career.

But her experience also included sexual harassment and assault. Those experiences contributed to a military life that was very different from the one she had expected.

She left after three years. A knee injury sustained during training continued to affect her, alongside other lasting effects of her time in uniform.

An earlier claim completed on her own

Years after leaving the military, a friend asked whether Leanne had applied for benefits related to her knee.

She had not realized that it was something she could pursue.

Leanne completed that claim herself and received compensation. But she remembers the work involved: forms, appointments, requests for more information and repeated visits to her doctor.

The approval did not mean she understood every support available to her. For years, she continued paying for some treatments without knowing that coverage might be connected to her accepted condition.

“I had no idea I had coverage for my knee.”

A referral became her first meeting

Leanne met us while sponsoring the Valley Maple Leafs. Her husband initially saw the connection as an opportunity for her to refer military clients and friends.

As Leanne spoke with a benefits navigator, the conversation became personal.

She had participated in a settlement process related to sexual misconduct during service and had assumed that was the extent of the support available. The discussion helped her understand that disability benefits and related healthcare supports raised different questions.

At a meeting in Dartmouth, we listened to her experiences and identified areas to examine further. She valued having room to decide how much she wanted to share.

“If you’re comfortable talking about more, you can. But if you’re not, you’re also not required to.”

One manageable step at a time

Leanne was busy with her business. She could not make appointments and applications her entire working week.

We discussed the broader picture, then helped her concentrate on individual claims and assessments as she was able to proceed.

“They’ve just been guiding me through each one separately, and it takes time.”

She also valued being able to ask questions as new information emerged. Our conversations helped her learn about treatment coverage and other benefits she had not previously understood.

Some assessments did not lead to an award. She still valued having help arranging them and understanding the process.

The relationship gave her a point of contact, a clearer direction and room to move at a manageable pace.

Looking beyond an immediate payment

Leanne talks about compensation, but also about counselling, treatment and the reassurance of understanding what support is available.

Her concerns extend into the future. She thinks about how her knee may affect everyday activities as she gets older and what help she may eventually need.

The most important change in her account is awareness. She no longer feels she is discovering support only by accident. She has people she can ask, and a clearer understanding of how to take the next step.

“I felt looked after.”
