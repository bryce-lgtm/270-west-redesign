I was 15 when I joined the Prince Edward Island Regiment band. I’d been a trumpet player in cadets since I was 12, and the military band director asked me to join. Over more than 50 years I served as a musician, a cook, an admin assistant to a wing chief warrant officer, and a cadet instructor officer. How many people get paid to play music? I loved the job, even if not every one of those years was good.

My back was the problem. I hurt it in Germany, had two surgeries, and left the regular force in 1994. I put in a claim that same year and it was denied, because the incident never made it onto my medical file. I appealed. Denied. I appealed again. Denied, and told I had no more appeals left. A review after that ended the same way. Nobody helped me, nobody asked me a question, and I gave up. I assumed that was the end of it.

Last year I was at my sister’s place talking to her nephew, and I mentioned my back and my time in the military. He knew people who look after veterans and offered to pass on my email. Someone from 270 West contacted me, and in November 2025 I sat down with Alex.

Right away he told me I could say whatever I wanted, and there was no obligation to do anything. We talked for an hour about my life in the military, my back, my mobility, my breathing. Then the appointments started. I didn’t even have a family doctor, and I had no idea the government would pay for the assessments. Alex told me not to worry about any of it, so I just went where I was told to go.

By June I had two claims approved, and a third came in July. My knees, my back, my hearing. Alex had my permission to monitor my VAC account, because I’m computer illiterate and it takes me half an hour just to get logged in. He would call me when something changed. And the file from 1994 that I thought was dead was reopened, without me doing a thing.

One of the claims was psychological. When I served, being gay in the military could cost you your job, and I spent years hiding it. I couldn’t invite my partner to a Christmas party. It affected me emotionally and psychologically, and my career suffered for it. I told the psychologist and psychiatrist the truth, and VAC recognized it. For the next two years I’m seeing a psychologist once a month, and I have an appointment this afternoon.

The fee was 10% plus tax, and it was explained before anything was filed. Ten per cent of nothing is nothing. Because of the claims, I’m free of debt, and I know I don’t owe anybody. That peace of mind is huge at 70. I’ve since brought my brother and my next-door neighbour to Alex, and a good friend of mine had already been through 270 West too.

I wouldn’t have done any of it myself. You’ve got to let people do what they do.
