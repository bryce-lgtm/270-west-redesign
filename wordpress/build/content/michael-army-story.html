Music brought Michael into military life. As a young cadet and trumpet player, he found a path that allowed him to serve while doing something he loved.

His career went on to include work as a musician, cook, administrative assistant and cadet officer across a period of more than 50 years.

“I loved being a cook. I loved looking after the young fellas and making sure they got fed well.”

He remembers learning how things worked behind the scenes and making friendships that lasted decades. He also carries experiences he was unable to speak openly about at the time.

A long history of service and unanswered questions

Michael lived with significant back problems and underwent two surgeries. A claim he submitted in the 1990s was denied, and further attempts did not resolve the matter.

Years later, he was still managing pain and mobility limitations. But he did not know what else he could pursue or how to return to a process that had already been so discouraging.

A family conversation eventually led to a referral to us. Someone offered to pass along his contact details, and we reached out to arrange a meeting.

A first meeting without pressure

Michael met with Alex, a benefit navigator, and spoke about his military life, back problems, mobility, breathing and other concerns.

The first meeting was a conversation. Alex explained that they could discuss what support was possible and what it would involve before Michael decided whether to proceed.

We subsequently arranged medical assessments. Michael had been without a family doctor when our relationship began, so knowing who to see and how to organise the appointments had been a significant obstacle.

With those arrangements made, he could concentrate on attending and explaining his experience.

The part of his service he had kept private

Michael also spoke about being gay during a period when he feared that being open about his sexuality could cost him his military career.

He describes hiding relationships, being unable to include his partner in social events and hearing hostile comments.

“I couldn’t take my partner to a Christmas party.”

Those restrictions affected ordinary parts of his life, as well as his emotional well-being. Their effects continued to trouble him long afterward.

When he met with mental health professionals, he was able to discuss that history and what it had meant for him.

“I told them everything. I just told them the truth.”

His account makes an important distinction: the concern was not his sexuality. It was the harm he experienced while feeling he had to conceal his life to protect his career.

A mental health claim was subsequently approved, and he began receiving ongoing psychological support.

Progress, with some matters still open

Michael described approvals for several claims, including one that was only partly approved.

His longstanding back claim had been reopened and remained in progress. A tinnitus claim was also still pending.

Alex continued to monitor developments and explain updates. Michael found that particularly helpful because accessing and using the online account was difficult for him.

The support gave him a person to contact rather than leaving him to work out each change alone.

Greater security and the confidence to recommend help

Michael says the financial outcome brought greater security for him and his spouse. Just as importantly, the mental health support gave him somewhere to discuss experiences that still affected his life.

His confidence in the relationship is reflected in the people he has referred: friends, a neighbour and his own brother.

“If I didn’t think the process was great, I certainly wouldn’t be recommending it to my own brother.”

Michael’s story holds pride in his service alongside the difficult parts. He loved much of the work and the friendships. With support, he was also able to speak about what had hurt him and pursue help for the effects that remained.
